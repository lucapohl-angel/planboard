# Websites, landing pages and content hierarchy

Use for marketing sites, business pages, shops and campaign pages. Research checked 2026-10-25.

Distinguish a multi-purpose homepage from a campaign landing page. Map audience intent and traffic source to the promise, evidence and next action. Match the promise in the originating ad or link. A focused campaign can emphasize one conversion; a business site may legitimately need navigation, contact and several visitor tasks. Do not apply “remove navigation” mechanically to every website. Use real proof, not fabricated testimonials or figures. [Unbounce landing-page guidance](https://unbounce.com/landing-page-articles/landing-page-best-practices/).

Before implementation, inventory pages and content owners. For each proposed section specify: visitor question, purpose, actual content needed, evidence/asset source and next action. Order sections according to the audience’s questions and objections, rather than automatically generating hero/features/testimonials/FAQ. Remove sections that repeat a job already done.

Use scale, contrast and grouping to establish the intended reading order. Check the page at a glance and at mobile width; a desktop arrangement does not prescribe mobile reading order. [NN/g visual hierarchy](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/).

Use a sitemap for page relationships and a section breakdown for one page. Plan checks for comprehension, task completion, contact/checkout behavior and responsive reading order. Conversion claims require evidence; a layout heuristic alone does not prove higher sales.
